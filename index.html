1. The Arkansas, Colorado, Platte, and Rio Grande all begin in Colorado.
  2. The Colorado River supplies more than 40 million people in seven states and Mexico. As of Sept. 20, 2026
  3. Denver Water reports natural streamflow ran at 22% of normal from April through July, the lowest in 110 years. As of Oct. 16, 2026
  4. Lake Powell and Lake Mead have reached record-low combined storage. As of Oct. 16, 2026
  5. Water from these four systems crosses 10 states.
  6. On August 21, the Interior Department set a two-year plan for 2027–2028: mandatory Lower Basin cuts of 1.25 million acre-feet a year, voluntary participation upstream. As of Oct. 16, 2026
  7. On August 26, Denver Water ended lawn watering after September 30, likely through spring 2027. Hand-watering of trees and gardens is still allowed. As of Oct. 16, 2026
  8. On September 4, the Colorado PUC cut Xcel's gas rate increase from the $190 million requested to $122.7 million a year, effective October 6. As of Oct. 16, 2026
  9. Three exposures: the interstate compact, the utility, the local supply. A ratepayer carries the second whatever happens to the first.

Everyone downstream of these decisions is affected: people who drink this water, farm with it, heat homes with the gas, and pay the bills. This is the record.
